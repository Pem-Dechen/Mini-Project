Divide the work among 4 people

Person 1: Problem Identification and Literature Review
Research and report writing


Responsibilities
- Choose and explain the real-world business problem.
- Explain why the problem is important.
- Explain why sequential data is suitable.
- Find and review relevant research papers.
- Explain existing methods and their limitations.
- Add proper citations and references.
Deliverable: Introduction, business problem, objectives, and literature review.



Person 2: Dataset Collection and Preparation
Data handling and explanation


Responsibilities
- Find and download an appropriate dataset.
- Record the dataset source and number of records.
- Explain the features and target variable.
- Clean missing, duplicate, or incorrect values where necessary.
- Prepare the sequential data for the model.
- Explain how the data is split into training, validation, and test sets.
Deliverable: Dataset file, preprocessing code, data description, and explanation of data preparation.



Person 3: Sequence Model Development
Main coding responsibility


Responsibilities
- Choose an appropriate RNN, LSTM, or GRU model.
- Explain why the selected model suits the problem.
- Write the model training code.
- Train the model using Person 2's prepared dataset.
- Save the trained model and generate predictions.
- Record training results and any challenges.
Deliverable: Working model code, trained model, predictions, and model-selection explanation.



Person 4: Evaluation, Analysis and Recommendations
Testing and business interpretation


Responsibilities
- Evaluate the trained model using suitable metrics.
- Prepare results tables, graphs, and a confusion matrix if appropriate.
- Explain the model's performance and sequential patterns.
- Identify the model's limitations.
- Explain how a business could use the findings.
- Write the conclusion and practical recommendations.
- Combine everyone's sections into the final report.
Deliverable: Evaluation results, interpretation, conclusion, recommendations, and compiled report.

Mini Project: 20%
Students will work in groups of maximum 3 members. Each group must identify a real-world
Business Intelligence problem involving sequential or time-dependent data and develop a
sequence-model-based solution. Your project should demonstrate how sequential data can be
collected, analysed, modelled, evaluated, and interpreted to support business decision-making.
The project should involve:
1. Identification of a real-world business problem.
2. Collection or selection of an appropriate dataset.
3. Literature review of related work.
4. Selection and implementation of an appropriate sequence model.
5. Evaluation of the model using suitable performance measures.
6. Analysis and interpretation of the results from a business perspective.
7. Recommendations based on the findings.
Students may select one of the following contexts:
Topic 1: Customer Churn Prediction
A business wants to identify customers who are likely to leave its service. Using historical
customer behaviour or interaction data, develop a sequence model to predict customer churn.
Students should:
• Identify sequential patterns in customer behaviour.
• Obtain a suitable customer dataset.
• Develop an RNN, LSTM, or GRU model.
• Train and evaluate the model.
• Identify customers who are at risk of churn.
• Explain how the business could use the results to improve customer retention.
Topic 2: Time Series Forecasting
A business wants to predict future sales, demand, market trends, or resource utilisation using
historical sequential data. Develop an appropriate sequence model to forecast future values.
Students should:
• Identify an appropriate time-series dataset.
• Explain the sequential nature of the data.
• Develop an RNN, LSTM, or GRU model.
• Forecast future values.
• Evaluate the model using appropriate measures such as MAE, MSE, RMSE, or MAPE.
• Explain how the forecast can support business decision-making.

Topic 3: Anomaly Detection
A business wants to identify unusual patterns in sequential data that may indicate fraud,
equipment failure, or other abnormal business activities. Develop a sequence-based approach to
detect anomalies.
Students may select one of the following contexts:
• Financial transaction fraud
• Machine/sensor failure
• Network activity
• Unusual customer transactions
• Other suitable business-related sequential data
Students should:
• Identify normal and unusual patterns.
• Prepare an appropriate sequential dataset.
• Develop an RNN, LSTM, or GRU-based approach.
• Identify potential anomalies.
• Evaluate the results.
• Explain how anomaly detection could help the business.
Topic 4: Text Analysis and Sentiment Prediction
A business receives customer reviews or social-media comments and wants to understand
whether customers have positive, negative, or neutral opinions. Develop a sequence-based model
to perform sentiment prediction.
Students should:
• Obtain a suitable customer review or text dataset.
• Explain why text can be treated as sequential data.
• Prepare the text data appropriately.
• Develop an RNN, LSTM, or GRU model.
• Predict customer sentiment.
• Evaluate the model.
• Analyse the results and provide business recommendations.
Topic 5: Recommendation System
An online business wants to recommend products, services, or content to customers based on
their previous behaviour. Using historical customer behaviour or interaction sequences, develop
a basic sequence-based recommendation approach.
Students should:
• Identify customer behaviour sequences.
• Analyse previous customer interactions.
• Develop an appropriate sequence-based approach.
• Generate personalised recommendations.
• Evaluate the recommendations using an appropriate approach.
• Explain how recommendations could improve customer engagement or sales.
